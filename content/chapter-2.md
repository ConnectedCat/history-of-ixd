In this chapter we will discuss the key developments in interaction design before the invention of a computer, meaning roughly before the beginning of the 19th century. People thought and tinkered with their instruments, tools and information systems since the beginnings of humanity. It hasn't always been a structured and deliberate process, but it is clear that the considerations of ease of use, cognitive load, aesthetic enjoyment, and later standardized modularity were consciously pursued.

We will trace the key developmentsa along the following lines: 
- Devices for information retrieval and storage, 
- Calculation and computation aids, 
- Tools, weapons, scientific, musical and other instruments

## Antiquity (BCE)

### The development of the early information systems
People started marking bones and cave walls in the pre-history, before the develpment of agriculture and the rigidly hierarchical societies, but it is the develpment of the pictographic writing itself that marks the recording of human history. The first systems of writing developed as [cuneiform in Sumer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform) around 3400 BCE and as [hieroglyphs in Egipt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_hieroglyphs) around 3200 BCE.

Writing developed independently several times in various places. Oracle Bone script was developed in China around 1200 BCE, and Mesoamerical writing system emerged around 900 BCE. Indus Valley civilization developed Indus script around 2500 BCE, but it remains undecifered, therefore it is still under debate whether it represents writing.

Writing represents on the earliest human information systems, establishing a consistent culture-dependent semantic respresentation in a form of symbol-meaning mapping. The associated practices engendered consistent input conventions (e.g. stylus shape) and medium of representation. In that way it layed the foundation concepts of user interface, which were developed over the course of millenia accompanied by the changes in the writing systems themselves.

It is worth noting that writing developed alongside with the development of social structures like property, ruling hierarchy, bureaucracy and the accompanying technologies, like agriculture. From the early age the technoglogies, incuding the technology of writing and information are deeply intertwined with the social structures that enable and depend on them.

### The development of numeration and number systems

Numeracy proliferated along with the development of literacy. Early societies adopted several numbering systems, so based on 5-count, some based on 10-count (decimal), some based on 12. The reasons for the adoption of a given system are foundational to the culture that adopted it. Ancient Egypt favored 10-base because of the number of fingers on both hands. One hypothesis places ancient Sumer on the intersection of the two earlier cultures - one using 5-base (number of fingers on one hand) and the other using 12-base (easily divisible by many numbers, also the number of phalanges on a hand excluding a thumb). This merger gave rise to the Sumerian and later Babylonian 60-based system (5x12) the remnants of which are with us to this day in 60 seconds, 60 minutes, 24 hours (12x2) and 360 degrees and days in a year (roughly). The Mesopotamian cultures were centered on trade (and the divisibility of 12 became very useful) and agriculture (for which the calendar and the time keeping are paramount.) With the development of these concerns the representational vocabulary was well established and easily borrowed by the later cultures.

In connection with the 60-base system an ingenious finger counting system was devised where one hand's phalanges (excluding the thumb) provided the base numbers and the other hand's fingers and thumb were used as multipliers.

Hand-counting systems present a unique example of a use of a human body as a technolgy, and self-referrential interface loop that allowed them to develop into systems of remarkable speed and accuracy, like Korean [Chisanbop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chisanbop). Despite the speed and convenience of the internal representation of calculations the expernal systems allow us to outsource our memory - the ability that became fundamental to coping with the increasing cognitive load of information processing. Follow the early pre-historic use of [tally bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishango_bone) an abacus was first inroduced in ancient Sumer and then then widely adopted across the ancient world.
